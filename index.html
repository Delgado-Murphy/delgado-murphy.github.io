Hasler · D&MBern 1964

A 1964 Swiss radio, playing again.

Press a key, hear music. The piano keys, the two knobs, the pilot lamp and the original speaker all work as they did. Inside, it streams radio from around the world, Spotify and YouTube Music, and it is a speaker for your phone.

From the telephone line to your Wi-Fi

Built to bring music home. It still does.

The Hasler HM was made in Bern around 1964 for Telefónica’s Hilo Musical, a service that sent music into homes over the telephone line. Its piano keys chose one of six channels.

The service is long gone. The radio isn’t. We kept everything you touch and hear, and gave it new music behind the same keys.

Why we restored it rather than replace it

What it does

Everything a radio should, nothing it shouldn’t.

Press a key, hear music

No phone, no app. Nearly 600 stations from about 120 countries, each one played on a Hasler before it went in, and playlists from Spotify and YouTube Music.

A speaker for your phone

Spotify, AirPlay, Bluetooth and other music apps find it as Hasler. Guests on your Wi-Fi can play too.

A lamp that talks

Amber for the radio, blue for a phone, a still ember when asleep, dark when it is safe to unplug. Its language.

Several rooms, in step

Two or more Haslers can play the same music together. Each keeps its own volume and tone.

What we hold to

A radio, not a subscription.

It works like the old radio

From the outside nothing changed: keys, knobs, lamp and speaker. The tone knob still switches it off. You never need a screen to listen.

No account, nothing in the cloud

There is nothing to sign up for with us. Stations, keys and settings live on the radio, and it keeps playing even if we disappear.

It tells you the truth

Plain words on its page and in its guide. When something is wrong, it says what, and one thing to try. When it can’t keep a promise, it says that too.

Your things stay yours

Updates never touch your keys or settings. Nothing about what you listen to is sent to us. Telling us how the radio is stays off until you switch it on.

Made to be repaired

Standard parts, an open board, open code and a build guide anyone can follow. The old cabinet’s parts are protected, not replaced.

Made by two people

Delgado & Murphy restore the cabinets, write the software, draw the board and answer when an owner writes.

The cabinets

Two radios, one heart.

Playing

Hasler HM

123456PU

Bern, around 1964. Six numbered keys and a PU key that spring back, a volume knob with a ceiling that protects the old speaker, and a tone knob that also switches it off.

  • Tap a key for its station or playlist; tap again to sleep or skip.
  • Hold 5 or 6 for volume; hold PU to pair a phone.
