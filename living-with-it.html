Living with a Hasler

You already know how to use it.

It works like the old radio: press a key, and music plays. Everything else is there for when you want more.

With your hands

Keys, knobs and a lamp.

The keys

Each numbered key plays its own station or playlist. Tap the playing station again and the radio sleeps; tap a playing playlist to skip a song. Hold 5 or 6 for volume. PU steps through the sources; hold it to pair a phone.

The knobs

Volume works for everything it plays, and never goes past a ceiling that protects the 1964 speaker. Tone shapes the sound, and turned left until it clicks, it puts the radio to sleep. Keep it there five seconds and it shuts down.

The lamp

Amber is the radio, blue is a phone, a still ember is asleep, green is OK, red is something wrong. Dark is safe to unplug. Every signal.

On your phone, if you like

The key page

On any phone or computer on the same Wi-Fi, open hasler.local. It looks like the radio: a dial that shows what is playing, the keys, the volume.

Browse nearly 600 stations by region, genre or language, search YouTube Music and Spotify, and put anything you find on a key. Set a sleep timer. Print a little card that says what each key does.

A new radio walks you through Make it yours, in the order of a first evening: the speaker’s ceiling, your keys, the room’s name, a phone.

Phones and rooms

Your music, through its speaker.

A speaker for every phone

In Spotify or AirPlay, choose Hasler. For Bluetooth, hold PU until the lamp breathes blue. Guests on your Wi-Fi can play their own Spotify without ever replacing yours. Tap any numbered key to take the radio back; when a phone stops, the radio returns to its own music by itself.

Music in several rooms

With more than one Hasler, give each a room name. On one radio’s page, tap Also play on… and tick the others: they play together, in step, each at its own volume.

Starter sets

Ten sets, by who’s listening.

A radio comes with keys that show what it can do. Pick a whole set in one go (Edit keys, then Start from a set…), then make it yours one key at a time. The seventh is for the Excelsior’s Tape key.

Questions people ask

Straight answers.

Does it need an account?

No. There is no Hasler account and nothing to register. Radio stations play without signing in to anything. Spotify keys use a Spotify Premium account you give the radio, and signing in to YouTube Music is only for your Liked songs and private playlists.

What if the makers’ servers disappear?

The radio doesn’t need us to play. Stations, playlists, phones and rooms all keep working, on the version it has. After about two weeks its Updates page says there is no fresh update information, and that is all. The one thing you can’t do without us is make a new memory card, which is why every radio comes with a spare.

Does it need the internet?

For radio stations, Spotify and YouTube Music, yes. Without the internet, a phone can still play to it over Bluetooth, and the lamp tells you the connection is down with two red flashes every few seconds.

Does it send anything about me?

Not unless you ask it to. Tell the makers how the radio is starts off; switched on, it sends a few lines once a day about whether everything works: no names, no places, nothing about what you listen to, and See what it sends shows exactly what. A note about a broken station stays on your radio.

Will an update break it?

Updates install only while it rests, check the radio afterwards, and go back to the previous version by themselves if anything is worse. You can also go back by hand. Your keys and settings are never changed by an update.

Do I need Home Assistant?

No. Everything works without it. If you have it, the radio can appear there as a media player, and a key can run a scene or turn on a light.

Can it hurt the old speaker?

The volume has a ceiling, set for that speaker, and nothing goes past it: not the knob, not a phone, not the tone knob’s effects.
